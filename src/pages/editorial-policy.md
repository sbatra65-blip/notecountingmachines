The [news](/news/) and [guides](/blog/) on this site help shops, petrol pumps, jewellers and offices across India understand counterfeit currency and choose the right note counting machine. This page explains how we write them, so you can judge how far to rely on them.

## Who we are

The content is published by **Infinite Solutions**, New Delhi, which sells and services note counting machines. We have a commercial interest: articles may recommend machines we sell, and each article says which machine it suggests. We never let that change the facts we report.

## Where our facts come from

- **Official sources first:** the Reserve Bank of India (rbi.org.in, paisaboltahai.rbi.org.in), the Press Information Bureau, the police and investigating agencies, and other countries' central banks and law-enforcement agencies.
- **Established news organisations** such as national newspapers, PTI, Reuters, AP and the BBC.
- **Manufacturers' published specifications** for machine features.

Every figure in a news article is linked to its source in the **Sources** list at the end of that article. If we cannot find a reliable source for a figure, we do not publish it.

## How we write

- We report facts in our own words and link the original. We quote at most one short sentence from any article.
- We keep a neutral tone about countries, governments and agencies, and keep reporting separate from advice. Practical advice for shops appears under its own heading.
- Each article shows the date it was published and, when facts change, the date it was updated.

## How we use AI

Our weekly world counterfeit currency round-up, in English and Hindi, is researched and drafted with the help of AI tools under these rules. Every figure is linked to the source it came from. Our team is responsible for everything we publish and corrects errors promptly.

## What we will never publish

- **Images of real banknotes.** Making documents that resemble currency notes is an offence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (formerly IPC section 489E). We use text, tables and plain illustrations instead. For pictures of security features, we link to RBI's own material.
- **How counterfeits are made, printed or obtained.** We only explain how fakes are detected and how to stay safe.
- **Unverified claims, rumours or speculation presented as fact.**

## Corrections

If you spot a mistake, email **notecountingmachines@zohomail.in** with the page link and the correction. We check it against the source, fix the article, and update its date.
