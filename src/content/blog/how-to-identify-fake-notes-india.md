Every Indian banknote in the Mahatma Gandhi (New) Series carries the same set of security markings. Learn what each one is and where it sits, and you can check any note in about ten seconds using three things: **tilt, light and touch**.

## The markings, and how to check each one

| Marking | Where it is | How to check | What a fake often shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Security thread** | Vertical, right of the portrait, with भारत and RBI | Tilt: on ₹100, ₹200 and ₹500 it shifts green → blue | Printed line that doesn't change colour or show the words |
| **Colour-changing numeral** | Denomination at bottom right (₹200, ₹500) | Tilt: green → blue | Flat green ink |
| **Watermark** | Blank area on the right | Hold to light: Gandhi portrait plus an electrotype number | Missing, or printed on the surface (visible without light) |
| **See-through register** | Small floral pattern on the left | Hold to light: front and back halves form the full number | Halves don't line up |
| **Latent image** | Vertical band right of the portrait | Hold flat at eye level: the denomination appears | Nothing appears |
| **Micro letters** | Near the portrait | Look closely or use a magnifier: RBI, भारत, India and the value | A blurred line |
| **Raised (intaglio) print** | Portrait, Ashoka Pillar, identification mark, angular lines on edges | Run a finger over them | Flat and smooth |
| **Number panel** | Top left and bottom right | Digits grow from small to large | Same-size digits |
| **Back of the note** | Reverse | Year of printing, Swachh Bharat logo, language panel, motif | Wrong motif or blurry print |

## Check the size and colour first

Toy notes and photocopies are often the wrong size or a slightly off colour. Keep a genuine note of the same value next to you and compare.

| Note | Colour | Size (mm) | Motif on the back |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹10 | Chocolate brown | 63 × 123 | Sun Temple, Konark |
| ₹20 | Greenish yellow | 63 × 129 | Ellora Caves |
| ₹50 | Fluorescent blue | 66 × 135 | Hampi with chariot |
| ₹100 | Lavender | 66 × 142 | Rani ki Vav |
| ₹200 | Bright yellow | 66 × 146 | Sanchi Stupa |
| ₹500 | Stone grey | 66 × 150 | Red Fort |

## Simple tools that help

- **A UV torch.** Under UV light, genuine note paper stays dull, while ordinary paper glows bright blue-white. It's a cheap first check for a small shop, not proof on its own.
- **A magnifier** for the micro letters.
- **A note counting machine** if you handle cash all day. It checks UV, magnetic (MG) and infrared (IR) ink on every note while counting, which no hand check can do. See [machine vs checking by hand](/blog/note-counting-machine-vs-checking-by-hand/).
