The ₹200 note gets less attention at the counter than the ₹500, but it carries the same security features, and the same tilt, light and touch checks work. Each takes a second or two.

## 1. Tilt: colour-shifting thread

The windowed security thread carries the words **भारत** and **RBI**. Tilt the note and it shifts from **green to blue**. A printed thread on a fake stays one colour.

## 2. Tilt again: the ₹200 numeral at bottom right

The numeral with the rupee symbol at the bottom right is in colour-changing ink and also moves from **green to blue**.

## 3. Hold to the light: watermark and see-through register

In the blank area you should see the **Mahatma Gandhi portrait and an electrotype 200 watermark**. The small **see-through register** shows the numeral 200 lining up perfectly from front and back.

## 4. Eye level: latent image

Hold the note flat at eye level. A hidden **200** appears in the vertical band to the right of the portrait.

## 5. Look closely: micro letters and number panel

Small letters reading **RBI, भारत, India and 200** sit near the portrait; on many fakes they are a blurred line. The serial numbers at top left and bottom right **grow from small to large**.

## 6. Feel it: raised print

Run a finger over the **portrait, the Ashoka Pillar emblem, the raised H mark** (with micro-text ₹200) and the **four angular lines with two small circles** on each edge. On a genuine note these are raised.

## Quick reference

| Check | Genuine ₹200 |
|---|---|
| Colour and size | Bright yellow, 66 mm × 146 mm |
| Thread (tilt) | Green to blue, with भारत and RBI |
| ₹200 numeral, bottom right (tilt) | Green to blue |
| Against light | Gandhi portrait + 200 watermark, see-through 200 |
| Back | Sanchi Stupa, Swachh Bharat logo, year of printing |
| Touch | Portrait, Ashoka Pillar, H mark and angular lines raised |

## When a machine is the better check

These checks take about 10 seconds a note. If you take dozens of ₹200 and ₹500 notes a day, a counter with UV, MG and IR sensors checks every note while counting it.
