This week's biggest story is from the outskirts of Hyderabad, where police seized fake ₹500 notes with a face value of ₹6.78 crore. Three more Indian seizures followed within days, and all of them involved ₹500, ₹200 or ₹100 notes, the denominations a busy shop counter handles every hour.

## Country by country

### India

**₹6.78 crore near Hyderabad.** On 1 November, police of the Future City Commissionerate stopped a car on NH-65 near Abdullapurmet and found ₹8 lakh in counterfeit notes, along with fake police ID cards and an imitation pistol. A follow-up search of a rented house turned up 134 bundles of fake ₹500 notes worth ₹6.7 crore. Four men were arrested and three are absconding, PTI reported on 3 November. Police say the gang also posed as police officers to rob people of genuine cash.

**Shravasti, Uttar Pradesh.** A joint UP STF and district police team raided a rice mill on 5 November and seized 804 fake notes (₹100, ₹200 and ₹500) with a face value of ₹1.74 lakh. Three men were arrested; police said the notes were meant for markets during the festive season.

**Delhi.** Police arrested two men from Bihar in Paharganj on 5 November with fake notes worth ₹2.65 lakh, most of them ₹200 notes.

**Shahdol, Madhya Pradesh.** Police arrested five men near Chirhati village with 1,803 fake notes (1,702 of ₹500 and 101 of ₹200), worth about ₹8.71 lakh, Amar Ujala reported on 7 November.

**How India is managing it:** state police and STF raids, NIA cases for organised rackets, and RBI rules requiring banks to machine-check notes and impound every fake they find.

### Colombia (with the US Secret Service)

Colombia's national police (DIJIN), with the Attorney General's office and support from the US Secret Service, dismantled a network in Sopetrán, Antioquia, in Operation Talero. Officers seized **US$601,777 in fake dollars** said to be ready for circulation, plus firearms, and arrested four people (Infobae, 3 November). Investigators are now tracing buyers and distributors.

**Approach:** cross-border cooperation. The US dollar is used worldwide, so the Secret Service works with foreign police to stop fakes before they travel.

### Senegal

On 10 October, the Dakar research brigade of Senegal's gendarmerie announced the seizure of fake notes worth about **1.1 billion CFA francs**: 6,600 counterfeit $100 bills, 10,000 counterfeit €100 notes and 1,200 "black" €100 notes. Six people were arrested across Dakar, Mbour, Thiès and Louga, according to the state news agency APS.

**Approach:** specialist gendarmerie units targeting networks that deal in foreign currency, a reminder that dollars and euros are faked well outside the US and Europe.

### Ghana

The Bank of Ghana moved the public unveiling of its new **Heritage Series** cedi notes to 12 November 2026, Graphic Online reported on 3 November. The series keeps all eight denominations (GH¢1 to GH¢200) but adds upgraded security features and better durability, replacing designs used for more than 20 years. Old notes remain legal tender, and the Governor asked people not to rush to exchange them.

**Approach:** redesigning notes regularly so counterfeiters have to chase new features.

### United Kingdom

A BBC explainer on 8 November, following a Nottingham taxi driver who spotted a fake £20 by its look and feel, set out the Bank of England numbers: about **2,00,000 fake notes** found in 2025 (face value £3.97 million), against 7,47,000 in 2012 before the move to polymer. Chief Cashier Rhys Phillips called counterfeits "very rare" and advised people never to risk their safety confronting someone over a suspect note.

**Approach:** polymer notes with see-through windows and holograms, plus simple "feel, look, tilt" public advice.

## What India can learn

- **New designs reset the game.** Ghana and the UK both upgraded notes to stay ahead of counterfeiters. Each new feature only helps if shops learn to check it.
- **Material matters.** UK fakes fell sharply after the move to polymer; India's notes are still paper, so checking at the counter carries more weight here.
- **Cooperation across borders works.** Colombia's case shows how sharing intelligence stops fakes before they reach shops.
- **Small seizures add up.** Three of this week's four Indian cases were under ₹10 lakh each, meant to be spent in local markets, one note at a time.

## What this means for your shop

- Run every ₹500 and ₹200 note through a counter with **UV, MG and IR** detection, even for small bills.
- Teach staff to check at least three features on any note the machine rejects: watermark, colour-shifting numeral and security thread. Our guide on [how to check a ₹500 note](/blog/how-to-check-500-rupee-note-security-features/) explains each one.
- Take extra care during the post-Diwali and wedding-season rush, when queues are long and checks get skipped.
- If you suspect a fake, politely refuse it and stay safe. Never pass it on.
