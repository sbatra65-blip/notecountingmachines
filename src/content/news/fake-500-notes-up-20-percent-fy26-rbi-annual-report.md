The Reserve Bank of India's annual report for 2025-26 shows counterfeit detections rising again, driven almost entirely by the ₹500 note. For a shop, petrol pump or jeweller that takes cash, the practical point is simple: the note you are most likely to be handed as a fake is also the one you handle most often.

## Counterfeit notes detected, by denomination

| Note | FY 2024-25 | FY 2025-26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹20 | 253 | 373 | +47.4% |
| ₹50 | 12,015 | 10,274 | −14.5% |
| ₹100 | 51,069 | 45,621 | −10.7% |
| ₹200 | 32,660 | 30,591 | −6.3% |
| ₹500 | 1,17,722 | 1,41,907 | +20.5% |
| ₹2,000 | 3,508 | 824 | −76.5% |
| **Total** | **2,17,396** | **2,29,746** | **+5.7%** |

Pieces detected in the banking sector, as reported from the RBI Annual Report 2025-26. Figures for ₹10 were not reported. Changes for ₹50 to ₹2,000 are our calculation from the reported numbers.

## Where the fakes were caught

Of the 2,29,746 counterfeits, other banks detected 2,24,334 (97.6%) and the Reserve Bank itself detected 5,412 (2.4%). In other words, nearly every fake in the table was found when someone deposited cash, after it had already changed hands at least once.

## Why this matters at your counter

A counterfeit found at a bank deposit is impounded and not credited, so the loss lands on whoever accepted it. Catching it at the counter, while the customer is still there, is the only point where you can refuse it.

The jump in ₹20 fakes is a warning too. Small notes are rarely checked, which is exactly why they are being copied.

## What to do

1. Check every ₹500 note, not just bundles from new customers.
2. Use a counter with at least UV, MG and IR detection; add MT or CIS if you take large cash payments.
3. Keep the machine's software updated so it recognises the latest note series.
4. Train staff on the [visible ₹500 security features](/blog/how-to-check-500-rupee-note-security-features/) for any note the machine flags.
