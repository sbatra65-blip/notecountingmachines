This week's biggest story is in India: the NIA made two more arrests in the case of fake notes worth ₹17.29 crore that were found inside a bank's own currency chest. If counterfeits can reach a currency chest, they can reach your cash drawer, which is why checking every ₹500 note at the counter still matters.

## Country by country

### India

**NIA case at a PNB currency chest.** Counterfeit notes with a face value of ₹17.29 crore were recovered from a Punjab National Bank currency chest in Civil Lines, Saharanpur (Uttar Pradesh). Local police registered the case on 29 August and the National Investigation Agency took it over on 3 September. After five arrests in September, the NIA arrested two more alleged key conspirators: one in Ambala on 16 October and one in Manali on 22 October. According to the PTI report, three bank employees allegedly involved have been suspended, and the agency says the probe is continuing.

**"Operation Noteflix" in Rajasthan.** Dungarpur police reported on 23 October that four more people, including the alleged mastermind, had been arrested after raids in several states, taking the total to six. The first two arrests, on 30 September, came with a seizure of fake notes worth ₹4.95 lakh.

**How India is managing it.** The RBI's circular of 31 July 2026 requires banks to check every banknote by machine, to fit note authentication or sorting machines in branches in border districts, and to train **all cash-handling staff on fake-note detection by 31 October 2026**, with reports due to RBI by 7 November. Counterfeits must always be impounded, never returned to the person who tendered them, and banks face penalties if they miss fakes in remittances, chest balances, ATMs or counter payments.

### Euro area and Germany

The ECB reported on 27 February 2026 that about 4,44,000 counterfeit euro notes were withdrawn in 2025, 20% fewer than the year before, or 14 fakes per million genuine notes. The €50 (53.2%) and €20 (27.0%) were the most copied. More recent figures from Germany's Bundesbank (7 August 2026) show 30,007 fake notes in the first half of 2026, down 4.3%, but €100 fakes rose 61%.

**Approach:** public "feel, look and tilt" checks, comparing a suspect note with a genuine one, and a new series of euro notes. The ECB shortlisted ten designs in July and expects to choose one around the end of 2026; current notes stay valid.

### United Kingdom

The Bank of England says about 2,00,000 counterfeit notes with a face value of £4 million were taken out of circulation in 2025, roughly double the 99,000 of 2024 (the lowest in over 30 years). Fakes are still below 0.0041% of notes in circulation.

**Approach:** polymer £5, £10, £20 and £50 notes with transparent windows and holograms, a free Banknote Checking Scheme for businesses, "Two Quick Checks" guidance, and checking posters in 11 languages. Suspected fakes go to the police, and the Bank does not reimburse counterfeits.

### Bangladesh

Bangladesh's Finance Ministry has drafted a Counterfeit Currency Prevention Act, 2026 (reported by Prothom Alo on 9 July). The draft proposes up to seven years in prison for knowingly holding or using fake notes and makes most offences non-bailable. It was opened for public comment and will go to the Cabinet after that; it is not yet law.

## What India can learn

- **Train the people who touch cash.** The RBI's 31 October training deadline for banks is similar to the UK's free checking scheme for shops. Training at the counter catches fakes earlier than training at the bank.
- **Watch the mid-value notes.** In Europe the €50 and €20 lead, and Germany's €100 fakes are rising. In India the ₹500 is the main target, so that is where the checks should be.
- **Machines plus people.** The RBI requires machine checks at banks, while the ECB and Bank of England push simple visual tests for the public. A shop needs both.
- **Clear laws help.** Bangladesh is moving towards tougher penalties; India already treats counterfeiting as a serious offence under the BNS.

## What this means for your shop

- Put every ₹500 note through a counter with at least **UV, MG and IR** detection, not just large bundles.
- Keep the machine's software updated so it recognises current note series.
- Teach staff the visible ₹500 security features for any note the machine rejects. Our guide on [how to check a ₹500 note](/blog/how-to-check-500-rupee-note-security-features/) walks through them.
- Never hand a suspect note back into circulation. Politely refuse it, and remember that a fake found at the bank is impounded without credit.
